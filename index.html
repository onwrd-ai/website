The approach

What not to buy

We name the money pits.

Most of what owners want "AI" for is really plumbing. Before any workflow gets an AI recommendation, it has to pass four questions. Routinely saying "that doesn't need AI at all" is why the rest gets trusted.

Is the input structured?

Same shape every time, or a different mess each day?

Is the output predictable?

Is there a right answer, or an acceptable range?

Could you write the rules down?

If you can, you probably don't need AI. You need automation.

Does it happen often enough?

Weekly hours, not an annual annoyance.

Fail two of the four and it's a money pit, or a plain-automation job. Either way, not an AI project.

Responsible by default

Right-sizing cuts cost and carbon with the same move.

Most small-business AI spend goes to oversized models doing undersized jobs. Choosing the smallest model that clears the bar lowers the bill and the footprint at once. It isn't a tax; it's the same discipline as good engineering.

  • 01

    Measure per useful outcome

    We use the Green Software Foundation's Software Carbon Intensity method: cost and carbon per outcome, not in total. Totals rise with growth; intensity shows improvement.

  • 02

    Route the plumbing to plumbing

    Roughly 60% of the workflows we find are deterministic. Sending those to ordinary automation instead of model calls is the biggest cost and footprint reduction available, and it's better engineering anyway.

  • 03

    Governance that fits a small business

    A one-page AI use policy, a shadow-AI inventory, data-handling rules for every connected tool, and a named owner. We work from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, with ISO/IEC 42001 as the proof if a client ever needs to show it.

  • 04

    Numbers, never claims

    We don't certify what we haven't measured. The efficiency story and the sustainability story are the same number, tracked beside time saved.

Questions

The honest answers.

Is my business too small for this?

We work with businesses from one person to a hundred. Smaller businesses often have the clearest wins, because one or two manual tasks make up most of the week. If there's nothing there, the call will tell you that for free.

Can't we just figure out AI ourselves?

Yes, and many owners have tried. Most stall because nobody fixed the workflow first, or because the tool came before the problem. The assessment isn't about knowing AI exists. It's about knowing which of your workflows it actually pays off in, and which it doesn't.

What does it ask of my team?

A 20-minute survey, one or two conversations with you, and 30–45 minutes each with a few of the people closest to the repetitive work. Nothing to install. We do the rest.

Do you use AI to do the analysis?

Yes, deliberately. AI helps us analyse interview transcripts and draft the report; a person interviews your team, checks every finding against the evidence, and decides what goes in. What AI can't do is notice what your staff have stopped complaining about.

Is our data safe?

Interviews are recorded for note-taking only, nothing is attributed to a team member by name, and the tools we use are business tiers that don't train on your data. We handle personal information under PIPEDA and BC's PIPA. Rule one we'll also leave with you: no client data in personal free accounts.

What if the report says we don't need AI?

Then it says so, and it tells you what you do need, which is usually a simpler process and some plain automation. Some of the best money we save clients is on the projects we tell them to skip.

What happens after the assessment?

The roadmap is yours either way. Many businesses run the first horizon themselves. If you'd rather have a partner, the Fractional AI Officer retainer picks the roadmap up one process at a time. No lock-in by design: the assessment stands alone.
