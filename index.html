The problem

Buying the tool is the easy part.

Owners are buying AI tools faster than anyone can work out which ones earn their keep. The tools and the prices change monthly, so the answer you researched last quarter is already out of date.

The harder problem is older than AI. Most of the processes being automated were already broken.

The approach

We fix the process before we automate it.

Everyone sells automation. The order is what makes it work. One process at a time, in this sequence, never skipped.

01

Audit

We watch your team do the work manually, end to end. The expensive steps are usually the ones nobody complains about any more.

02

Optimise

Cut the fat before touching a tool. A 15-step process is usually a 9-step process, and a shorter one is cheaper to run and easier to fix.

03

Automate

Only now, and only into something you can maintain yourself. Plain automation where no AI is needed, AI where it earns its place, people where judgement matters.

Automating a broken process just makes the inefficiency faster.

What not to buy

We name the money pits.

Most of what owners want AI for is really plumbing. Every workflow has to pass four questions before we would recommend AI for it.

Is the input structured?

Same shape every time, or a different mess each day?

Is the output predictable?

Is there a right answer, or an acceptable range?

Could you write the rules down?

If you can, you need automation, not AI.

Does it happen often enough?

Weekly hours, not an annual annoyance.

Fail two of the four and it's a money pit, or a plain-automation job. Either way, not an AI project.

Sustainable & responsible AI

Ask about our sustainable and responsible AI services.

Right-sizing cuts cost and carbon with the same move. If governance or footprint matters to your business, say so on the call and we'll scope it into the work.

  • 01

    Right-sizing

    Most AI spend goes to oversized models doing undersized jobs. We pick the smallest model that clears the bar and send the deterministic work to plain automation. The bill and the footprint fall together.

    Measurement

    Cost and carbon per useful outcome, using the Green Software Foundation's Software Carbon Intensity method. Totals rise with growth; intensity shows whether you're improving.

    A one-page AI use policy, a shadow-AI inventory, data-handling rules and a named owner. We work from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, with ISO/IEC 42001 if you ever need to prove it.

Questions

The honest answers.

Is my business too small for this?

We work with businesses from one person to a hundred. The smallest ones often have the clearest wins, because one or two manual tasks make up most of the week. If there's nothing there, the call will tell you that for free.

Can't we just figure out AI ourselves?

Plenty of owners have tried. Most stall because the tool came before the problem, or because nobody fixed the workflow first. The assessment isn't about knowing AI exists. It's about knowing which of your workflows it pays off in.

What does it ask of my team?

A 20-minute survey, one or two conversations with you, and 30–45 minutes each with a few of the people closest to the repetitive work. Nothing to install.

Do you use AI to do the analysis?

Yes, deliberately. AI helps us analyse the interview transcripts and draft the report. A person runs the interviews, checks every finding against the evidence, and decides what goes in.

Is our data safe?

Interviews are recorded for note-taking only, nothing is attributed to a team member by name, and we use business-tier tools that don't train on your data. We handle personal information under PIPEDA and BC's PIPA.

What if the report says we don't need AI?

Then it says so, and it tells you what you do need instead, which is usually a simpler process and some plain automation. Most of the money we save clients is on the projects we tell them to skip.

What happens after the assessment?

The roadmap is yours either way, and plenty of businesses run the first horizon themselves. If you'd rather have a partner, the Fractional AI Officer retainer picks it up one process at a time. The assessment stands alone by design.
